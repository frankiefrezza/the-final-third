The Basics
Anatomy of a Cigar

A premium handmade cigar is entirely crafted from whole tobacco leaves, meticulously assembled in three distinct layers:

  • Wrapper (Capa) : The outermost leaf. Flawless, oily, and highly flavorful. It accounts for up to 60% of the cigar's total flavor profile.
  • Binder (Capote) : The structural leaf that holds the filler tobacco tightly together and ensures a consistent, even burn.
  • Filler (Tripa) : The core blend of leaves (usually 3 to 5 different types) that dictate the strength and complexity of the cigar.
  • Cap & Foot: The cap is the enclosed head you cut before smoking. The foot is the open end you light.

Corona

5.5" - 6" x 42-44

The benchmark size against which all other sizes are measured.

Petit Corona

~4.5" x 40-42

A miniature corona.

Churchill

7" x 47

A large corona format, famously favored by Winston Churchill.

Robusto

4.75" - 5.5" x 48-52

A short, thick cigar.

Corona Gorda / Toro

5.625" - 6" x 46-50

A thicker corona that has grown immensely in popularity.

Double Corona

7.5" - 8.5" x 49-52

A very large, long-lasting format.

Panetela / Lancero

5" - 7.5" x 34-38

Long and thin elegance; maximizes wrapper flavor.

Lonsdale

6.5" x 42

Longer than a corona, thicker than a panetela.

Grande / Gordo

4.75"+ x 60+

Modern extra-thick sizes.

Belicoso

A short pyramid with a slightly rounded head.

Torpedo

Perfecto

Varies

Wrapper Leaves
Colors & Profiles
Wrappers vary widely in color due to the curing and fermentation processes.

Double Claro / Candela

Light green. Achieved by a quick drying process that locks in chlorophyll. Mild and grassy.

Claro

Light tan. Typically shade-grown under cheesecloth. Mild, smooth, and neutral.

Colorado Claro

Medium brown. Often sun-grown. Slightly stronger profile than Claro.

Colorado

Reddish-brown. Robust and rich flavor, usually shade-grown.

Colorado Maduro

Dark brown. A shade lighter than true Maduro, offering rich, aromatic spice.

Maduro

Very dark brown to black. Leaves are heavily fermented, resulting in sweet, rich flavors.

Oscuro

Black. The darkest wrapper, achieved by leaving the leaves on the plant longest and fermenting them intensely.

Preparation
Mastering the Cut

The goal is to open the cap without unravelling the wrapper. Always cut above the "shoulder" (the curved line where the cap ends).

Straight Cut The standard guillotine cut. Removes the cap entirely for a wide, open draw. Best for Torpedos.
V-Cut Creates a deep wedge. Concentrates the smoke on the palate while maintaining the structural integrity of the cap.
Cross-Axis Crown Cut Executed by making two perpendicular V-cuts to form a star shape. Offers an incredibly open draw with focused flavor delivery.
Punch Cut Bores a small circular hole. Best for larger ring gauges to tighten the draw. Avoid on torpedo/pointed shapes.
Ignition
The Art of Lighting
Never touch the flame to the tobacco

Hold the cigar at a 45-degree angle above the flame. Rotate it slowly to toast the foot until the entire surface is evenly charred. Only then, place it in your mouth and take gentle puffs while holding the flame near (but not touching) the foot to draw the heat in.

  • Soft Flame (Matches/Bic): Cooler burn, preserving delicate wrapper flavors. Must be used indoors away from wind. Let sulfur matches burn off entirely before toasting.
  • Torch Lighter: Wind-resistant and highly precise. Can scorch the tobacco if held too close. Keep the visible tip of the flame at least an inch away.
  • Cedar Spill: The traditional method. Igniting a strip of Spanish cedar provides a low-temperature flame that imparts a subtle, sweet aroma during the light.
Tasting Technique
Palate & Retrohaling

A cigar is traditionally divided into three sections, each providing a different experience as moisture and heat build up: the First Third (brightest flavors), Second Third (the balanced "sweet spot"), and The Final Third (deep, intense, and robust).

How to Retrohale

Much of a cigar's flavor profile is actually perceived through olfactory sensors (smell). Retrohaling is the act of expelling a small amount of smoke through your nasal cavity, rather than just exhaling through the mouth.

  • Draw the smoke into your mouth (do not inhale into your lungs).
  • Release about 70-80% of the smoke through your mouth normally.
  • With your mouth closed, push the remaining 20-30% of the smoke up and out through your nose using your throat and tongue.
Storage
Acclimation & Resting

When you purchase cigars—especially online where they endure temperature fluctuations during shipping—they experience "transit shock." Letting them rest allows humidity to stabilize evenly, ensuring a straight burn.

The 70/70 Rule vs. 65/65 Historically, 70°F and 70% Relative Humidity (RH) was the standard. However, modern enthusiasts generally prefer a slightly lower environment around 65°F and 65% RH, which prevents mold and provides a sharper, more reliable burn.
Cellophane: On or Off?

Cellophane is porous. Leaving it on protects the wrapper from physical damage while rummaging through your humidor and slows down aging slightly. Taking it off accelerates the aging process and allows aromas to marry with other cigars. Either choice is entirely acceptable.

Wellbeing
Preventing Cigar Sickness

Cigar sickness (nausea, cold sweats, or dizziness) is caused by a sudden influx of nicotine, which acts as an alkaline and triggers a rapid drop in blood sugar levels.

  • Prevention: Never smoke a full-bodied cigar on an empty stomach. A hearty meal beforehand is your best defense. Keep your pacing to about one puff per minute so the cigar doesn't overheat and release excess nicotine.
  • The Cure: If you feel green, you need to raise your blood sugar quickly. A packet of pure sugar, a sugary soda, or a piece of chocolate will neutralize the alkaline effects and relieve the nausea within minutes.
